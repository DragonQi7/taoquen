Your Five Element Qi Prism
Qi Intelligence Suite · Tool 1

The Prism of Qi

When white light passes through a prism whole, all five colours appear in balance. When one is missing — the light is incomplete.

Background

In Traditional Chinese Medicine, the Five Elements — Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, Water — are not metaphors. They are a map of how energy moves through the body. Every organ, emotion, season, and symptom belongs to one of the five. When one element weakens, the entire cycle is affected — because the elements feed each other in sequence.

Concept

The Qi Prism is a live model of your five-element balance. Each element has two states: Balanced — Qi flows freely. Depleted — the organ is signalling. Drag any element's slider and watch the nourishing cycle respond in real time. This is not a test. It is a mirror — showing you how connected the system actually is.

How to Use It

Start with the element that feels most familiar — the one whose symptoms you recognise. Adjust its Qi level and observe which organs light up downstream. Use this after each week of the programme to track which elements are shifting. The Qigong Practice button shows you how movement changes the picture.

This tool is built on the Five Element Consciousness Framework as taught in Dragon's Way Qigong® by Grand Master Nan Lu. For educational and reflective use — not a clinical diagnosis.

Your Body Clock✓ Included in your programme
Qi Intelligence Suite · Tool 2

The Qi Body Clock

Your body is not running on a single clock. It is running on twelve — one for each organ, each with its own two-hour peak.

Background

In TCM, Qi moves through the twelve organ meridians in a precise 24-hour cycle. Each organ receives its peak Qi flow during a two-hour window. This is not theory — it is the reason you wake at 3am, feel heavy after lunch, or get your clearest thinking at a specific time of day. The body is telling you which organ is active.

Concept

The Body Clock shows you the live state of your organ clock — which organ is at peak Qi right now, what it governs, and what signal it sends when it is underperforming. Each organ on the clock carries an emotional and physical message. When you feel something at a specific hour, this tool gives it a name.

How to Use It

Open this tool at the time of day when you feel a recurring symptom — fatigue, waking, digestive discomfort, low mood. Tap the organ that is currently at peak. Read what it governs and what it is asking for. Use this weekly during the programme to build a personal pattern map of which organs are consistently signalling.

The organ clock is a foundational principle of TCM taught across all Dragon's Way Qigong® sessions. Patterns you notice here will align directly with the Five Element work in the programme.

Your Qi Bank✓ Included in your programme
Qi Intelligence Suite · Tool 3

The Qi Bank

You have two energy accounts. Most people only know about one — and they have been overdrawing the other for years.

Background

TCM describes two types of Qi: Post-natal Qi — the energy you generate daily through food, breath, sleep, and movement. And Pre-natal Qi — the deep constitutional reserve you were born with, stored in the Kidneys. Post-natal Qi is your checking account. Pre-natal Qi is your savings. When daily life depletes the checking account, the body quietly begins drawing from savings.

Concept

The Qi Bank helps you see where your energy is actually coming from — and whether you are building reserves or spending them. Most people living with chronic fatigue, adrenal exhaustion, or persistent low energy are drawing heavily on Pre-natal Qi without knowing it. This tool makes that pattern visible and gives you a framework to reverse it.

How to Use It

Use this tool at the end of each programme week. Reflect on your sleep quality, energy on waking, food choices, and emotional output. Adjust the sliders honestly. The balance shown is not a score — it is a conversation starter. Use it to guide which eating-for-healing and Qigong practices to prioritise in the coming week.

Building Pre-natal Qi reserves is a central goal of Dragon's Way Qigong®. The Kidney-strengthening movements and seasonal eating recommendations in the programme work directly on this account.

Two Frameworks, One Body✓ Included in your programme
Qi Intelligence Suite · Tool 4

Two Frameworks, One Body

Western medicine and Traditional Chinese Medicine are not in disagreement. They are looking at the same body through two completely different lenses.

Background

Western medicine maps the body through structure — organs, cells, biochemical pathways, measurable markers. TCM maps the body through function and energy — how Qi flows, where it stagnates, how the elements interact. Neither is wrong. But for people whose tests come back normal while they still feel unwell, only one of these frameworks has an answer for them.

Concept

This tool places both frameworks side by side so you can see how they interpret the same symptoms differently. A Western view of fatigue and a TCM view of Kidney Qi deficiency are describing the same person — but only one of them has a protocol for addressing the root. Understanding both frameworks makes you a more informed participant in your own healing.

How to Use It

Choose a symptom or condition you are working with. See how each framework names it, what it looks for, and what it recommends. Use this as a reference during the programme when something you are experiencing does not yet have a Western explanation — but does have a TCM one. It is a literacy tool, not a diagnostic.

Understanding why TCM works the way it does deepens your practice. Grand Master Nan Lu often says: the body is smarter than any system we have built to explain it. Both lenses together get closer to the truth.

Can You Digest the Universe?✓ Included in your programme
Qi Intelligence Suite · Tool 5

Can You Digest the Universe?

In TCM, digestion is not just what happens in the stomach. It is the body's capacity to transform everything it takes in — food, information, emotion, experience.

Background

The Spleen and Stomach are the central digestive organs in TCM — the Earth element. They govern transformation and transportation: extracting what is useful and releasing what is not. But in the TCM view, this applies to more than food. Worry, overthinking, and unprocessed emotion all tax the same system. A weakened Earth element shows up as bloating, fatigue after meals, brain fog, and difficulty letting go.

Concept

This tool maps the five-stage digestive pipeline — from intake to transformation to release — and shows where the process breaks down for most people. Each stage corresponds to a specific organ pair, emotion, and type of input. When digestion fails at any stage, the body signals. This tool helps you identify where your pipeline is strongest and where it needs support.

How to Use It

Work through the five stages and note which ones feel familiar. Pay attention to both physical and emotional symptoms — they map to the same organs. Use the eating-for-healing guidance from the programme alongside this tool to see which foods and practices address your specific stage of breakdown. Return to it seasonally — the Earth element is most vulnerable in late summer.

The Spleen-Stomach partnership is one of the most commonly addressed organ pairs in Dragon's Way Qigong®. The wu ji standing posture and warming foods recommended in the programme directly nourish this system.

Cold & Dampness Check✓ Included in your programme
Qi Intelligence Suite · Tool 6

Cold & Dampness Check

Two of the most common hidden patterns in modern health — and two of the least recognised by conventional medicine.

Background

In TCM, Cold and Dampness are not just weather conditions — they are internal pathogenic patterns that accumulate over time through diet, lifestyle, and environment. Cold contracts and slows — leading to stiffness, poor circulation, and low metabolic energy. Dampness obstructs and weighs — leading to heaviness, sluggishness, brain fog, and fluid retention. Most people carry both, often without realising it.

Concept

Cold and Dampness are two of the six external pathogens in TCM that can become internalised — meaning they take up residence in the body's channels and organs. Once internal, they interfere with Qi flow, digestion, and organ communication. They are not dramatic in the early stages — which is precisely why they go unaddressed for so long. This tool helps you identify whether either pattern is present and how deeply it has settled.

How to Use It

Work through the self-check by rating each symptom honestly. The results will indicate whether your pattern leans Cold, Damp, or both — and how strong that pattern currently is. Use the recommendations alongside your programme eating plan. Cold patterns respond to warming foods and Yang-building Qigong. Damp patterns respond to dry, cooked foods and movement that circulates Qi through the Spleen meridian.

Clearing Cold and Dampness is one of the underlying aims of the Dragon's Way Qigong® eating-for-healing protocol. Pay attention to how your pattern score shifts as you progress through the six weeks.
